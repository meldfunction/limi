Who We Are

We are a practice studio focused on attending to what's alive in our communities and organizations. We create, we consult, and we cultivate spaces for receptivity and dialogue to make room for deeper engagement through creativity, regeneration, and awareness.

Our work moves across the personal, the interpersonal, and the systemic, so that we can work and live more fully together.

Two Practices, One Studio

Where the work takes shape.

Limicelia holds two practices as a single unit — worked together, not run as separate ventures.

Practice One

Understory

Weaving what's beneath the surface into shared understanding.

For over a decade, this began as research — studying how meaning, trust, and communication move differently across cultures inside organizations. That research became a question that outgrew the academy: what actually lets a group understand itself? Understory grew from asking it differently: facilitating spaces — contemplative, creative, and strategic, held together rather than apart — where people and groups learn to sense what's actually happening beneath the surface of how they work.

  • Practice cohorts — small, sustained groups who bring a real challenge from their own work and stay with it together over weeks.
  • Open practice sessions — free, drop-in gatherings for anyone curious what it's like to sense what's actually alive in their work and life.
  • Dialogue and listening circles — deep-listening formats where a group learns to stay with disagreement rather than resolve it too quickly.
  • Writing circles — shaping personal or organizational experience into shared language a team can work with together.
  • Collective sensemaking circles — structured conversation across real differences, long enough for its shape to change.
  • Contemplative & awareness practice — individual and group practice for presence, attention, and self-understanding.
Practice Two

Fieldwork

Where communities build what they need.

Fieldwork gets early-stage, community-rooted work the room and resources it needs before anyone else will bet on it. Most funding shows up after an idea has already proven itself — we're interested in before, hosting cooperative hackathons and community-led tech events, and offering the first, smallest checks and spaces to people building regenerative ventures.

  • Seed support for emerging practice — small, early checks and hosting at the point where an idea needs room more than resources.
  • Entrepreneurial incubation — light, hands-on support for people building regenerative ventures.
  • Community tech convenings — cooperative hackathons and build-days where local groups and technologists work on shared problems together.
  • Warm data gatherings — holding the relationships and context a spreadsheet can't capture.
  • Published writing & media — essays, audio, and film that carry the work into public conversation.
